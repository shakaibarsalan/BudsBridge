**Bridge mode: hear your phone and your laptop in the same buds.** The buds stay connected to the
laptop, the phone plays through the laptop as if it were a Bluetooth speaker, and Windows mixes
both into the buds. No switching, and nothing to install on the phone.

This is a **pre-release**: device listing has been confirmed on a real laptop, but switching and
bridge mode still need their run on real buds and a real phone. The
[hardware checklist](https://github.com/shakaibarsalan/BudsBridge/blob/main/docs/HARDWARE_TESTING.md)
lists what to try (section 3 covers bridge mode).

### Download

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `BudsBridge.exe` | Single file, no installer. Download and open it |
| `BudsBridge-0.2.0-windows-x64-setup.exe` | Installer for your user only: Start menu entry and uninstaller, no admin prompt |
| `SHA256SUMS.txt` | Checksums for both files |

Requirements: Windows 11, buds paired with the laptop, and for bridge mode the phone paired with the
laptop too (**Settings → Bluetooth & devices → Add device**). Nothing else to install. The build isn't
code-signed, so SmartScreen warns once: **More info → Run anyway**.

### New

- **Phone audio** panel: switch on *Hear … in my buds* for a paired phone. It keeps the phone
  connected, waits and reconnects when the phone goes away, and comes back on after a restart.
- Tray menu items to turn phone audio on and off; the tray tooltip shows when it's playing.
- Device names no longer carry Windows' "Hands-Free" suffix ("IGNITE" instead of "IGNITE Hands-Free").
- The hotkey field can't be overwritten while you're typing in it.

### Known limits

- Phone audio arrives about 0.2–0.4 s late (two Bluetooth hops): fine for music and podcasts, visible
  as lip-sync drift if you watch a video on the phone's own screen.
- One Bluetooth radio carrying two audio streams can stutter on some laptops.
- It's dual connectivity *through the laptop*: away from the laptop, hand the buds back to the phone
  (one click or `Ctrl+Alt+B`).
- Phone calls go through Microsoft Phone Link and use Bluetooth's lower-quality hands-free audio.
